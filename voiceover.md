## Chapter 1: Open

It's Founder Tip Tuesday. When you hand off a big project, the review you schedule decides how much you can still change. Stripe scheduled two, one at twenty percent done and one at eighty.

## Chapter 2: Two reviews at Stripe

Krithika Shankarraman was Stripe's first marketing hire, and she told First Round Review how it worked. Those two reviews were part of how Stripe kept its standards as the company grew and the work spread across more people.

## Chapter 3: The 20 percent check

The twenty percent review came before anybody had built much. It covered the goal and the intent, so everyone agreed on what they were making while that was still cheap to change.

## Chapter 4: The 80 percent check

The eighty percent review looked at the execution, with enough time left to act on what it found. At ninety-nine, a review can only approve what's already there.

## Chapter 5: If you build hardware

If you build hardware, ninety-nine percent tends to be the day the drawings go out for tooling. A change after that means cutting new tooling, and that's money and weeks nobody planned for.

## Chapter 6: The author reads first

Those reviews are the founder's half of a handoff. Will Larson, who runs engineering at Imprint, wrote in July about the other half, the person who did the work. His rule is that the author reads their own work before anybody else does. He's writing about work drafted with A I, where a first pass costs almost nothing. He says the biggest cultural failure with these tools is when the person receiving the work can tell they're the first human to read it.

## Chapter 7: Larson's own analysis

Larson was the first reader on one of his own analyses. He pulled incident data with an A I agent, and the trend didn't match what he'd seen. The tool only counted incidents that got tagged to a team when the alert fired, and that left out about half of them. Because he read it before he acted on it, he didn't send his team after a problem that didn't exist.

## Chapter 8: This week

This week, pick one project you've already handed off. Book a review for when it's about a fifth done and another when it's most of the way there. Then ask whoever owns it to read their own work before they send it to you, including anything a tool drafted for them.
