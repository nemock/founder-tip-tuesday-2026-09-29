# Founder Tip Tuesday: Week of September 29, 2026

**Live deck:** https://nemock.github.io/founder-tip-tuesday-2026-09-29/

Krithika Shankarraman was Stripe's first marketing hire, and she told First Round Review how
the company kept its standards as it grew: any big project got two reviews. The first came
at about 20 percent done and covered only the goal and the intent. The second came at 80
percent and looked at the execution, while there was still time to act on what it found. At
99 percent, a review can only approve what's already there, and at a hardware company 99
percent tends to be the day the drawings go out for tooling. Will Larson, who runs
engineering at Imprint, wrote the rule for the other side of the handoff in July: the author
must be the first human in the loop for their own output. He was the first reader on his own
incident analysis, pulled with an AI agent, and caught that it left out about half the
incidents because nobody had tagged them to a team. This week, pick one project you've handed
off, book a review at about a fifth done and another most of the way there, and ask whoever
owns it to read their own work before it reaches you.

## Slide outline

1. Stripe checked every big project at 20% and at 80%.
2. Two reviews on every big project.
3. At 20%, agree on the goal and the intent.
4. At 80%, there's still time to act.
5. 99% is the day the drawings go out for tooling.
6. "The author must be the first human in the loop for their output." (Will Larson)
7. About half of the incidents were missing.
8. He read it before he acted on it.
9. Pick one project you've handed off.
10. Which of my own handoffs am I going to see for the first time at 99%?
